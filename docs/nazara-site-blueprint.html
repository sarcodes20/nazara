Wireframes here are drawings, not mockups.

Solid bars are type at their stated token. Nothing in a wireframe is a colour proposal — colour comes from Volume One.

Notation used throughout

  • c2–c7 — the columns an element occupies on the 12-column desktop grid.
  • 100vh, 140vh — the scroll length a section occupies, not its visual height.
  • ease-stone / ease-cut / ease-lift — the three curves from Volume One §07. No fourth curve exists.
  • Curtain — the 1200 ms clip-path image wipe with 1.06 → 1.00 counter-scale.
  • Mason's line — the 800 ms per-line headline reveal, 60 ms stagger.
  • Rule ignites — a 1 px Backlight line growing left-to-right over 400 ms.
Scope note — read before building

Volume One's primary navigation carries five destinations: Library, Works, Provenance, Atelier, Journal. This volume specifies eight pages, of which Atelier and Journal are not included — they remain in the navigation as designed and are deferred to a later release; the build should ship them as single-template pages using the Provenance and Works layouts respectively. Conversely, Architects and Downloads are specified here in full but do not take navigation slots: they are a trade door, reached from the footer, the Library mega menu, and the foot of every stone page. Contact is not a navigation item either — it is the persistent Request a viewing action that sits in the header of every page.

Architecture & navigation

Sitemap, header, mega menu, drawer, the Selection, transitions, loading.

Five doors, one action, and a tray that follows you.

The information architecture solves one problem: two audiences arriving at the same URL with opposite needs. A principal wants to be shown something. An architect wants to find something and leave with a file. The site serves the first with its main sequence and the second with a trade door that is never more than one click away, and it never asks anyone which one they are.

Sitemap
  • /  Home — the sequence. One job: make the visitor want to look at stone for longer than they intended.
  • /library — the Library index (Collections). Filterable, comparable, two view modes. → /library/[stone] Stone detail (Collection detail) → /library/[stone]/[block] Block detail, deep link from the slab index.
  • /works — Works index (Projects) → /works/[project] Work detail.
  • /provenance — About: the word, the manifesto, the quarries, Makrana, the atelier, the refusal.
  • /atelier · /journal — in navigation, deferred (see §00).
  • /architects — the trade door → /architects/downloads The Index · /architects/samples · /architects/specify
  • /viewing — Request a viewing (Contact). Also the destination of the header action from every page.
  • /selection — the visitor's own tray of stones, shareable by link, exportable as PDF.

The Selection — the tray

A persistent, anonymous tray. Add to selection appears on every stone card and stone page. The header shows a mono count once it is non-empty: SELECTION 4. Clicking slides a Viewing Room panel in from the right over 480 ms ease-lift, 440 px wide, holding each stone at 1.684 : 1 with its spec line and a remove control. From the tray: Download as PDF, Share by link, Request a viewing of these four — which pre-fills the form. This is the conversion mechanic of the whole site and it never once uses the word cart, basket or wishlist.

Footer — identical on every page

Exactly as specified in Volume One §06: Viewing Room, 200 px top padding on desktop, wordmark at 44 px, one sentence of place, the Journal sign-up, Navigate and Studio columns, and the coordinate colophon. The only per-page variation permitted is a single contextual Rule link above the wordmark — on a stone page, For architects →; on a work, Stones used in this project →. Nothing else changes, ever.

Page transitions

A Viewing Room panel wipes up from the bottom edge over 600 ms ease-lift, covering the viewport. The incoming route renders behind it, scrolled to top. The panel then wipes off the top edge over 700 ms ease-stone. Total 1300 ms, of which the middle 200 ms is a full black hold — long enough to feel like a room change, short enough not to be a wait. The wordmark does not appear in the panel; the loader is the only place it does. Back and forward use the identical transition — direction is never reversed, because a room change is not undoable.

Eyebrow: THE LONG LOOK

We wet it. — Water shows a slab its own colour. Dry stone lies about what it will become.

We rake it. — A light held almost flat against the face is the only way crystal, saw-scoring and the true depth of a vein will read.

We turn it. — The same block gives a different drawing on every axis. Vein-cut is a landscape. Cross-cut is a bloom.

Closing line, after the third: "Then we take it, or we leave it on the bench. There is no third option."

Interactions

  • Filter click — the option's rule ignites, the count crossfades over 120 ms, the grid re-flows with a FLIP transition, 480 ms ease-stone. Cards that survive move; cards that leave fade out over 200 ms first so the layout never jumps under the cursor.
  • Card hover — image 1.00 → 1.03 over 700 ms, Backlight rule grows above the caption over 500 ms, cursor expands to the 78 px VIEW ring.
  • Add to selection — a small Rule control appears at the card's bottom-right on hover only. On click the label swaps to IN SELECTION in Quarried, and the header's Selection count crossfades. No animation flies across the screen.
  • Typology hover — the hovered tile holds full opacity, the rest of the wall drops to 55% over 400 ms ease-cut. This is the single most persuasive interaction on the site: it is exactly what a person does with their hands at a slab yard.
  • View toggle — the grid does not reload. Cards morph into tiles with a FLIP transition over 600 ms ease-stone while captions fade out; reversing brings them back. The scroll position is preserved.
  • Keyboard — arrow keys move a 1 px Backlight focus ring through the grid; Enter opens; Space adds to selection; / focuses filter search. The whole page is operable without a mouse, because half this audience uses a laptop trackpad in a site meeting.
  • Empty — type only: "No stone matches those six filters. The nearest is Makrana Albeta." with the nearest stone's card beneath it.
Scrolling

Plain inertial scroll, damping 0.08. No parallax anywhere on this page — parallax on a comparison grid is actively hostile. The header collapses at 400 px; the filter rail sticks at 120 px; nothing else moves independently.

CTA placement

No Ink button on this page at all. The primary action is Add to selection, distributed across every card, and the header's Request a viewing is always present. A page whose job is browsing does not interrupt browsing.

Mobile

Filters collapse into a single sticky bar: FILTER (2) · SORT · VIEW. Tapping opens a full-height Viewing Room sheet from the bottom, 480 ms ease-lift, with the six groups as accordions and a fixed Show 17 stones button at the base. Cards go 1-up; typology goes 3-up.

Expected user emotion

Competence, then covetousness. The architect should feel the tool was built by someone who has actually specified stone; the principal should feel they are looking through a private index rather than a shop. Nobody should feel sold to on this page — the selling happened on the homepage and happens again on the stone page.

Reduced motion — the complete substitution

Mobile doctrine

4 columns, 20 px margin, 64 px header, and three things that simply do not exist.

Global rules

  • No custom cursor, no parallax, no ambient scale. All three are desktop ideas. Mobile keeps the curtain and the mason's line, which is enough.
  • Section rhythm drops to 64 px and type follows the mobile column of the scale in Volume One §04. Display-l is 48 px, not a scaled-down 120.
  • Buttons go full width at 52 px — the single documented exception to the desktop rule.
  • Sticky bottom bars exist on exactly two pages: Stone detail (Add to selection / Request a viewing) and Downloads (the tray). Nowhere else.
  • Every table scrolls horizontally inside its own container. The page body never scrolls sideways.
  • Tap targets are 44 px minimum regardless of the visual size of the control.
  • Drawer, not dropdown. Full-screen Viewing Room, Bodoni 34 items, 60 ms stagger, closing reverses it.
  • Sheets, not modals. Filters and sorting arrive as bottom sheets on ease-lift with a fixed action button at the base stating the result: Show 17 stones.
