01

Brand Story

The name, the philosophy, the voice, the position.

A nazara is not scenery.
It is the thing you stop walking to look at.

नज़ारा · نظارہ — Hindi and Urdu, from the Arabic naẓar, "the gaze." The word does not describe a landscape. It describes the act of beholding one. It is a word about the viewer as much as the view.

Marble is the only building material that exists to be looked at. Its structural case is weak; its optical case is total. A slab is a record of pressure, water, heat and a hundred million years of dark — a drawing made without an audience, by a hand with no intention. Nazara's craft is not manufacture. It is selection: seeing what is there, deciding what it is for, and orienting it so a room can hold it.

Manifesto — the founding text. Reproduced verbatim; never paraphrased, never shortened.

Nothing here was designed.
Everything here was chosen.

A block comes out of the hill in the dark. We wet it, rake a light across it, turn it, and look for a long time. Then we take it, or we leave it on the bench. There is no third option, and no amount of money changes the answer.

The Four Principles

I · The Long Look
Nothing enters the library on a photograph. Every block is seen wet, dry, raked and lit before it is bought. The look takes as long as it takes; slowness is the product, not a bottleneck in it.
II · The Named Block
Every slab carries its block number, quarry, bench, cut date and its siblings' whereabouts. Traceability is the luxury. A slab with no history is a surface; a slab with one is a work.
III · The Room Decides
Stone is specified for a room, never for a catalogue. We fabricate to the drawing, dry-lay the bookmatch on the floor of the atelier, photograph it, and only then crate it.
IV · We Say No
Nazara turns down work: wrong stone, wrong room, wrong reason. Refusal is a service, and it is the only credible proof that the yes means something.

Emotions to produce

  • Stillness — the pace of a gallery, not a showroom.
  • Weight — everything on screen should feel like it has mass and consequence.
  • Patience — reveals are slow; nothing competes for attention.
  • Recognition — the specialist audience should feel addressed as peers.
  • Restraint — the brand is never eager. It does not chase.

Emotions to avoid

  • Urgency — no countdowns, no "limited stock," no pop-ups.
  • Abundance — never brag about volume, range or country count.
  • Value — no price signals of any kind, ever.
  • Friendliness — warmth comes from craft, not from tone of service.
  • Nationalism — India is a fact of provenance, never a flag.

Tone of voice — register: a museum wall label crossed with a shipping manifest

Sentence discipline: headlines 4–12 words, no subordinate clauses. Body paragraphs of two sentences maximum on marketing pages. Technical copy uses the trade's own vocabulary without glossing it — gangsaw, bookmatch, quadmatch, vein-cut, fleuri, leathered, resin-backed, first choice — because the audience specifies stone for a living. Banned words, permanently: exquisite, epitome, unparalleled, world-class, one-stop, elevate, curated (as adjective), timeless, luxurious, opulent, bespoke, journey.

Luxury positioning

The category competes on two axes and everybody crowds the same corner: more stones, from more countries, faster. Nazara declines the axis entirely. Where StoneX sells mastery (the maker) and Classic Marble sells variety (the catalogue), Nazara sells the gaze (the beholder) — and prices access to it.

Audience · Primary

Architects and interior designers specifying for a single named project. They arrive with a drawing and a deadline. Give them dimensions, finishes, lead times and slab-level availability without asking them to enquire first.

Audience · Principal

UHNW private clients and their project managers. They arrive with a reference image and no vocabulary. Give them the story of the block, the room it suits, and the sense that they were shown something not everyone is shown.

Audience · Volume

Hospitality groups, luxury developers, premium builders. They arrive with a quantity and a repeat requirement. Give them continuity of supply from one block family, and a private index of what is reserved for them.

02

Moodboard

Materials, texture, light, architecture, composition, references.

The room is dark. One thing in it is lit.

Everything below descends from a single image: a slab standing upright in an unlit room, wet, with one raking light on it and a person standing far enough back to see the whole thing at once.

Materials — the permitted set

  • Makrana calcite — warm white, the stone of the Taj Mahal, quarried 90 km from the studio. The house white.
  • Verde Guatemala serpentine — the green-black that gives the dark palette its undertone.
  • Honey onyx, backlit — the only material permitted to emit light. Source of the accent.
  • Unfilled travertine — for texture close-ups, never for hero imagery.
  • Unlacquered brass — allowed to brown. Never polished gold, never bright.
  • Blackened steel · sandblasted glass · lime plaster · oiled walnut · raw linen · jute — the supporting cast for any set dressing, in that order of preference.

Textures — what the camera is actually looking for

  • Gangsaw striation — the fine parallel scoring left by the blade on an unpolished face.
  • The wet edge — the dark advancing line as water is poured down a dry slab.
  • Chalk on raw block — the quarryman's hand-written block number.
  • The 45° mitre — the joint that makes stone look solid rather than clad.
  • The shadow gap — 12 mm, the detail that lets a stone plane float.
  • Dust on a sill — Kishangarh's white marble dust settles on everything. Do not clean it out of the frame.
Lighting doctrine
  • Raking, 15°–25° — light skimmed across the surface is the only way crystal and saw-texture read. Default for all stone.
  • North light — for interiors. Cool, directional, unglamorous, permanent.
  • One pool in a dark room — the Viewing Room condition. Falloff to true black at the frame edge.
  • Transmitted — light through onyx or 20 mm marble. Rare, and the only warm light permitted.
  • Blue hour — exterior and architecture, the 20 minutes before sunrise, with warm practicals inside.
  • Never — on-axis flash, fluorescent showroom tubes, mixed colour temperature, light with no visible direction.
Architecture the brand lives inside
  • Peter Zumthor · Therme Vals — stone as stratification; mass and water.
  • Mies van der Rohe · Barcelona Pavilion — the golden onyx wall. The original backlit slab.
  • Carlo Scarpa · Brion, Olivetti — the step, the joint, the shadow gap, the reverence for an edge.
  • Geoffrey Bawa — architecture as a sequence of framed views. The literal translation of the name.
  • Studio Mumbai · Bijoy Jain — Indian material craft at world register. The proof it need not read as export trade.
  • John Pawson · Vincent Van Duysen — the void, and warmth without ornament.

Composition rules

  • One subject per frame. If two things are interesting, that is two photographs.
  • Stone occupies ≤ 60% of an interior frame. Negative space is the price of looking expensive.
  • One straight edge always runs parallel to the frame. No accidental tilt, ever.
  • Verticals are vertical. Tilt-shift or corrected in post. Non-negotiable.
  • The horizon sits on a grid line — 1/3 or 1/2, never between.
  • Asymmetry over centring in layout; dead-centre only for the slab typology (§08).

Visual references — photography and print

  • Hélène Binet — the definitive reference for architectural light and shadow.
  • Edward Burtynsky · Carrara Marble Quarries — the quarry as landscape, seen from above.
  • Bernd & Hilla Becher — typology. The model for the slab library (§08).
  • Candida Höfer — the empty, symmetrical, unpeopled grand interior.
  • Hiroshi Sugimoto · Seascapes — the long look; a horizon and almost nothing else.
  • Print: Domus, Casabella, Cabana, The Gentlewoman. Brand: Aman, Loro Piana, Hermès windows, Boffi and Poliform catalogues.
Anti-references — if the work resembles any of these, it is wrong

Slabs shot in a lit warehouse with a forklift in frame. Marble on a pure white cutout background. Blue-orange teal grading. A rendered 3D "luxury villa" interior. Stock photography of handshakes, families or smiling staff. Gold gradients. Any typeface with a swash. The word "Since" over a sepia photograph. A flag. A rotating 3D slab viewer. Watermarks. Before/after sliders. Anything with a drop shadow on it.

03

Colour

Nine brand values, two grounds, one rule about the accent.

The stone supplies the colour.
The interface supplies the silence.

The palette is deliberately achromatic with a single warm accent, because every photograph in the system is a coloured object placed on it. A saturated interface would compete with the product it exists to frame. Two decisions carry the whole palette: the neutrals are biased green, not warm; and the accent is defined as light, not as pigment.

Primary — the two grounds

Viewing Room #0F1411
Primary brand colour · dark ground · footer · overlays · mega menu

Not black. A green-black taken from serpentine, four points of green above neutral. Against pure #000 the whites in a marble photograph go slightly blue and dead; against a green-black they read warmer and more luminous by simultaneous contrast. This is the single most consequential colour decision in the system, and it is optical, not decorative.

Quarry Dust #EAEBE4
Primary light ground · default page background

The pale green-grey of marble dust settled on every surface in Kishangarh. Chosen over the default warm cream because cream reads as heritage and hospitality; this reads as mineral and as a gallery wall. It also sits one step below pure white, so a white slab photographed on it still looks like the brightest thing on the page.

Backgrounds & surfaces — elevation is expressed by ground change, never by shadow

Makrana #F4F5F0
Light surface · specimen plates · filter rails · forms

Named for the calcite quarried 90 km from the studio. One step above the ground; used for any panel that should read as a sheet laid on the page.

Bone #FBFBF8
Highest light surface · modals · image mounts · dropdowns

The nearest the system comes to white. Reserved for the topmost layer, so that "raised" is legible without a single shadow being drawn.

Serpentine #171E19
Dark surface · cards and panels inside the Viewing Room

The dark-mode counterpart to Makrana. Same green bias, so a panel never reads as a grey patch floating on a green-black field.

Guatemala #1F2821
Highest dark surface · modals · hovered dark rows

Named for Verde Guatemala, the stone the whole dark family is sampled from. The hover state of every dark surface.

Text — four values, and no others

Nero #141814
Primary text on light · 15.9:1 on Quarry Dust

Two points lighter than the Viewing Room so that headline text on a light page and a dark panel are visibly different objects. Never pure black — pure black on a soft ground looks printed on, not set in.

Slate #565B52
Secondary text on light · body copy at length · 6.9:1

Long-form body sits here rather than at full Nero. It lowers the shout of a full page of text and lets headlines hold rank without going larger.

Ash #878C81
Tertiary · labels, specs, captions, placeholders · 4.6:1

The floor for text of any kind. Nothing in the system is set lighter than this on Quarry Dust, including disabled labels.

Chalk #E9EBE4
Primary text on dark · 15.6:1 on Viewing Room

Off-white, matched to the light ground so an inverted band reads as the same page turned over rather than a different site. Dark-mode secondary is #A5AA9E, tertiary #737969.

Accent — Backlight

Backlight #E0B278
Accent on dark grounds · hairlines, focus, glow, growing underlines

The colour of a 20 mm honey-onyx slab lit from behind — the one warm light the brand allows. The rule: Backlight is never a fill. It appears only as light — a 1 px rule that ignites, an underline that grows, a focus ring, a glow behind an image. No button, badge, chip, banner or block is ever this colour. That single restriction is what keeps a gold accent from reading as a gold accent.

Backlight Deep #B5822F
The same accent, on light grounds · rules, focus, hover underlines

#E0B278 disappears on Quarry Dust. This is its light-ground substitution, carrying the identical role. Substitution is automatic through the token; a designer never picks between them.

Backlight Ink #7A5620
The only accent value permitted for text on light · 6.1:1

Section numbers, hex values, the small mono line under the wordmark. Used sparingly enough that it registers as a marking system, not a colour scheme.

Lines — one weight, two strengths, no exceptions

Hairline #D2D4C9 / dark #2A322C
Default 1 px rule · table rows, section dividers, plate borders

The system separates things with lines and space, not with boxes and shadows. This is the workhorse of the entire interface.

Hairline Strong #B4B8AA / dark #3D473F
Interactive 1 px rule · input underlines, outline buttons

A line the user can act on is always darker than a line that merely divides. That difference alone tells the eye what is interactive.

Semantic — drawn from the same rock, never from a stock palette

Quarried · Success #4C6B52 / dark #86A98D
Confirmation · "In library" · viewing confirmed

A moss green sampled from the same serpentine family as the dark ground, so a success state never looks like it arrived from a different design system.

Fissure · Error #9A3B2E / dark #D98878
Form errors · destructive confirmations · "Reserved"

The oxide red of an iron fault line running through a block — the flaw a selector looks for. Muted deliberately: an error in this system is a correction, not an alarm.

On Hold · Warning #8A6B22 / dark #C79B45
"Last slab" · "On hold until…" · lead-time notices

Olive-gold, pushed away from Backlight in saturation and hue so the two are never confused. Warning is a state of stock; Backlight is a quality of light.

04

Typography

Three faces, three registers: poetry, practice, proof.

The hairline in a Didone is the vein in the stone.

The competition sets everything in one grotesque, which is why it reads corporate. Nazara uses three faces with strictly separated jobs. A high-contrast Didone carries the brand's voice, because its hairline-to-stem contrast is the same optical event as a white vein crossing dark marble. A neutral grotesque carries the studio's voice. A technical monospace carries the stone's own voice — every dimension, block number and coordinate in the system.

Display & Headline — Bodoni Moda · optical size axis 6–96 · weights 400, 500

Makrana, in daylight

Block 7741, cut on the eighteenth of March

Role
Every headline 32 px and above. Slab names. Pull quotes. The wordmark. Nothing else.
Case
Sentence case, always. Uppercase Didone reads as fashion masthead; sentence case reads as architectural monograph. The only uppercase Bodoni in the system is the wordmark itself.
Optical size
Set font-optical-sizing: auto. At 96 the hairlines thin correctly; forcing a low opsz at display size is the single most common way to make this face look cheap.
Italic
Permitted for dates, Latin stone names and single-line pull quotes. Never for a whole paragraph.
Fallback
"Bodoni Moda", Didot, "Bodoni MT", Georgia, "Times New Roman", serif
Body, UI, Navigation & Buttons — Archivo · weights 300, 400, 500, 600

A grotesque with square terminals and a large x-height, set light at size and regular at length.

Archivo holds its shape at 11 px in uppercase with 0.18 em of tracking — the condition every label in this system lives in — and it has no calligraphic tendency to argue with the Didone above it. That neutrality is the point: it is the voice of the studio speaking plainly underneath the voice of the brand.

The 32 px rule — the system's one memorable typographic law

Bodoni above 32 px. Archivo below. Plex for anything measurable.

Tracking tightens as size grows and loosens as it shrinks: display-l at −0.025 em, body at +0.005 em, label at +0.18 em. Negative tracking is never applied below 20 px.

Measure, rhythm and hierarchy

  • Measure — body copy 62 characters maximum, 45 minimum. Lead paragraphs 30 em. Never full-bleed text.
  • Paragraph spacing — 1 em of the element's own line-height, never a blank line.
  • Heading space — space above a heading is always 2× the space below it. This alone creates hierarchy without extra weight.
  • Widows — text-wrap: balance on every heading; no heading ends on a single word.
  • Hyphenation — off, everywhere, in every language.
  • Alignment — flush left, ragged right. Nothing is justified. Nothing is centred except the wordmark on the loading screen.
  • One display item per viewport — two Bodoni items above 46 px must never be visible at the same time.

Grid & Spacing

Twelve columns, an 8 px baseline, and one proportion taken from the stone itself.

Every image in the system is 1.684 : 1 — the true proportion of a gangsaw slab.

A quarried block is sawn into slabs of roughly 3200 × 1900 mm. That ratio, 1.684, is the brand's primary image format and appears nowhere else in the industry, because everybody else crops to 16:9. Using the material's own dimension as the layout constant is what makes the grid feel authored rather than chosen from a framework.

Placement rules — the asymmetry that makes it editorial

  • Text never starts at column 1. Body copy occupies columns 2–7 or 6–11. The empty first column is the brand's signature margin.
  • Headlines may break the grid left by one column, and only headlines.
  • Images are either full-bleed or gridded — never inset by a stray amount. Full-bleed images ignore the margin entirely.
  • Two-image pairs are unequal: 7 + 4 columns with a column of air between, never 6 + 6.
  • Nothing is centred except the loading wordmark and the slab typology photograph.
  • Baseline — an 8 px vertical grid governs all spacing. Type sits on it; images may break it.

06

Components

Zero radius, one border weight, almost no shadow.

Rectangles are cut. Circles are drilled.

Corner radius is 0 on every rectangle in the system — buttons, cards, inputs, modals, images, tags, tables. Stone is cut with a blade; a rounded corner is a moulding, and moulding is what plastic does. The only curve permitted is a full circle, used for exactly three things: the icon button, the custom cursor, and the scroll indicator. That is the whole geometry.

07

Motion

Weight, not bounce. Nothing in this system is springy.

Stone has mass. Motion must admit it.

Everything decelerates and nothing overshoots. A spring easing would tell the viewer the object is light, and every frame of this brand is arguing the opposite.

Loading — "the wet line." The signature animation of the brand.
Nazara

When water is poured down a dry slab, its true colour appears — this is how every stone in the world is judged before it is bought. The loading screen is that gesture: the wordmark sits in Ash on Viewing Room and fills with Chalk from the bottom up over 1600 ms on --ease-stone, holds for 400 ms, then the whole panel lifts away. It is the only centred element in the entire system.

Easing — three curves, no others
ease-stone
cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.30, 1) — reveals, image wipes, page transitions. Fast departure, long settle.
ease-cut
cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1) — state changes: hover, focus, colour, opacity.
ease-lift
cubic-bezier(0.33, 0, 0.00, 1) — panels and drawers entering. Slight hold at the start, like something heavy being moved.
Durations — a fixed ladder
TokenmsApplies to
instant120Colour, opacity, focus rings
state240Hover, nav bar height, tracking
rule400Backlight rules growing
element480Panels, mega menu, scrims
image700Card image scale on hover
reveal800Text lines entering
curtain1200Image wipes, hero entry

Nothing in the system is faster than 120 ms or slower than 1600 ms. Staggers are always 60 ms and never exceed eight items — beyond eight, the group animates as one.

How text appears — "the mason's line"

Headlines are split by line, each line masked by its own container. On entry the line translates from translateY(0.6em) to 0 with clip-path revealing from the baseline upward, 800 ms on ease-stone, 60 ms stagger between lines. Opacity moves 0 → 1 across the first 40% only, so the type never sits visibly half-faded. Body copy does not animate line-by-line — it fades and rises 12 px as one block over 600 ms. Labels and mono specs do not animate at all; they are already there when you arrive, like a caption on a wall.

How images appear — "the curtain"

Never a fade. An inset() clip-path opens from the left edge to full width over 1200 ms on ease-stone, while the image inside counter-scales 1.06 → 1.00 over the same interval. The result reads as a cloth being drawn off a slab rather than a picture loading. Full-bleed heroes use the same wipe from the bottom edge upward. Two images in the same viewport wipe 120 ms apart, never simultaneously.

Scrolling, transitions, restraint
  • Smooth scroll — inertial, damping 0.08, multiplier 1.0. Never faster than native, never bouncy.
  • Parallax — images only, 8–12% maximum. Text never parallaxes. No layer moves in the opposite direction to the scroll.
  • Page transitions — a Viewing Room panel wipes up over 600 ms (ease-lift), the new page loads behind it, the panel wipes off the top over 700 ms (ease-stone).
  • Scroll indicator — a 40 px circle with a 1 px stroke and a descending line inside, on the hero only, fading at 15% scroll.
  • Never — scroll-jacking, section snapping, horizontal scroll hijacks, counters that tick up, marquees, 3D tilt on cards, particle effects, video autoplay with sound.
  • Reduced motion — every reveal becomes a 200 ms opacity fade, parallax is 0, the custom cursor is off, the wet line holds filled, page transitions become instant. The site loses nothing legible.

Photography

Three registers, one grade, one immovable typology.

The shadows are green. The highlights are neutral. Nothing is warm except transmitted light.

That single grading instruction, applied consistently, will make a library of thousands of images by different photographers read as one brand — more reliably than any logo placement. It also matches the palette: the shadows fall toward Viewing Room, the whites hold at Makrana, and the only warmth on the page is Backlight, which by definition is light passing through stone.

The typology — the single most important rule in this section

Every slab entering the Library is photographed in exactly the same frame: standing upright against a Viewing Room wall, camera on a rail at the slab's vertical centre, sensor parallel to the face, two 4-foot softboxes at 45° left and right, no props, no floor visible, the same lens and the same distance every time.

Over a year this produces a Becher-style typology: hundreds of images that are identical in everything except the stone. Scrolled as a grid it becomes hypnotic, and it makes comparison — the specifier's actual job — trivially easy. No competitor can copy it retroactively, because it only works if you did it from the first slab.

Raking light — the standard setup

A key light almost parallel to the surface makes crystal, saw-scoring and vein relief visible. A black flag opposite keeps the shadow side from filling. This is the only lighting diagram the brand needs.

Mood & composition

  • Quiet — the frame should feel like a room someone has just left.
  • Cool, not cold — the green shadow keeps it from going clinical blue.
  • Scale is always readable — one element in an interior tells you how big the room is.
  • Weather is welcome — dust, haze, wet floors, low sun through a saw-shed.
  • People appear rarely and are always working, never presenting.
  • One straight edge parallel to the frame, in every photograph, without exception.

Forbidden, absolutely

  • HDR, clarity sliders, sky replacement, AI upscaling.
  • Teal-and-orange grading, heavy vignettes, tilt-shift miniature effects.
  • Stone cut out on white. Stone floating. Stone reflected on a black gloss plane.
  • Renders presented as photographs. If it is a render it is labelled RENDER in mono.
  • Stock imagery of any kind, including "luxury interior" libraries.
  • Watermarks, logos, borders, frames, or the wordmark burned into an image.

Iconography

Outline only. 1.25 px. Sharp corners. Drawn like an annotation, not an app icon.

The icons are architectural drawings, not symbols.

A filled icon reads as mass, and mass belongs to the photographs. Every icon in the system is a line drawing at a single stroke weight, constructed from straight lines and true circles, with butt caps and mitred joins — the same 0-radius logic as the components. The set is custom because the vocabulary is: no library has a glyph for a bookmatch or a vein-cut, and those are the icons this brand actually needs.

Handover notes for the build
  • Theme structure — the light set on bare :root; the Viewing Room set repeated three times: @media (prefers-color-scheme: dark), [data-theme="dark"], and .viewing-room for dark bands inside a light page.
  • Fonts — Bodoni Moda with font-optical-sizing: auto; subset Latin + Latin-Ext; display=swap; preload the display face only.
  • Images — AVIF with WebP fallback, srcset at 1×/2×, sized to the five fixed ratios at source. Never rely on CSS object-fit to invent a crop.
  • Motion — every transition reads its duration and easing from tokens. A hard-coded ms value anywhere in the codebase is a bug.
  • Reduced motion — one global block collapses all reveals to a 200 ms fade and disables the cursor, parallax and page transitions.
The five rules that must survive contact with the build
  • 1 · Radius is 0 on every rectangle. Circles only for the icon button, the cursor and the scroll indicator.
  • 2 · Backlight is never a fill — only a rule, a ring, a glow or a growing underline.
  • 3 · Every image is one of the five ratios, and the slab ratio is 1.684 : 1.
  • 4 · Every slab shown anywhere carries its mono spec line: family · dimensions · block number · slabs remaining.
  • 5 · 160 px between desktop sections. The emptiness is the product.
